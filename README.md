# Orientation 2089

**Live:** https://justinsteinmetz.github.io/WhereDoYouStand/

**Paired with:** [ARIA](https://justinsteinmetz.github.io/ARIA/) — a civilisation encounter

---

## What it is

A values instrument set in 2089. Twenty-seven concrete situations, no right answers.

The year is not decoration. 2089 is far enough that speculative technology — neural uplinks, synthetic citizens, cognitive enhancement, post-scarcity economics, planetary governance — is assumed rather than contested. Students cannot argue about whether these things will exist. They have to decide what they believe about them. The speculative distance is what makes the underlying values legible.

The Transition changed everything. It didn't settle the old arguments. It made them stranger, faster, and higher-stakes.

## The six tensions

| Code | Name | The question |
|---|---|---|
| FREE | Liberty & Control | When should systems constrain what minds can do? |
| ECON | Post-Scarcity & Merit | If abundance exists, who decides who gets it? |
| WORLD | Planetary & Local | Does civilisation need a single governance layer? |
| CHANGE | Upgrade & Continuity | How much of what we are should we keep? |
| COMM | Collective & Individual | When does the network's need outweigh the person's? |
| TRUST | Trust & Scrutiny | Do those in charge get the benefit of the doubt? |

---

## The 27 situations

**Liberty & Control (5)**
- City AI monitors all public communications to prevent coordinated violence
- Protocol to pause a citizen's neural uplink for 72 hours during civil emergency
- 16-year-old wants cognitive enhancement implants that will alter personality
- AI model publishes an accurate argument that a democratic government is structurally corrupt
- Synthetic mind applies for citizenship, claims sentience, refuses to provide proof

**Post-Scarcity & Merit (5)**
- Automated labour has made most jobs obsolete — universal income regardless of contribution?
- Pharmaceutical AI synthesises a cure — company wants to charge; others want it free
- Designer uploads and sells a consciousness template — intellectual property or shared resource?
- Off-world mining corporation earns more than most nations — planetary redistribution?
- Post-scarcity means food and shelter cost almost nothing — should luxury goods be rationed?

**Planetary & Local (5)**
- Climate intervention that cools the planet will damage monsoon seasons in South Asia
- Every nation surrenders veto power over planetary defence to a single AI-governed council
- A billion climate refugees want relocation rights to any habitable territory
- Martian colony of 80,000 wants full independence — Earth argues it funded the colony
- Shared language model has become the de facto global tongue — protect smaller languages?

**Upgrade & Continuity (4)**
- Memory editing allows people to erase trauma painlessly
- Civilisational archive project will preserve current human culture in digital amber
- Children enrolled in AI-tutored learning that triples cognitive development but removes childhood unstructured time
- Proposal to genetically edit humanity's descendants to remove the capacity for tribalism

**Collective & Individual (4)**
- Hive-mind commune of 10,000 functions as a single legal entity — members have no separate legal standing
- Partial memory sharing between willing strangers — but shared memories cannot be fully deleted
- Post-scarcity: is accumulation of vastly more social influence than average still a problem?
- A citizen's cognitive footprint is now a measurable resource — should it be taxed?

**Trust & Scrutiny (4)**
- Synthetic intelligence with 99.97% accuracy recommends a judicial sentence — human judge disagrees
- Distributed AI news system with no human editors — better factual record than any human publication
- Planetary governance council has prevented three extinction-level events — should it now govern permanently?
- Synthetic mind requests the right to vote, citing cognitive and ethical capabilities exceeding most humans

## How it works

Students face each situation and choose: one of two positions, or a middle ground (except on forced-choice questions, which have no middle option). Forced-choice questions are the ones that tell you the most.

At the end: a map of where the student lands across the six tensions, reflection questions tailored to their specific pattern of choices, and a "someone who'd disagree" debate prompt.

Unfamiliar terms are underlined throughout — tap to define. The glossary covers: synthetic citizen, cognitive equity, neural uplink, post-scarcity, substrate rights, and others.

---

## The handoff to ARIA

At the end of Orientation 2089, students are redirected to ARIA. Their orientation results are carried forward. ARIA then places them inside the 18 years (2071–2089) that produced the world they just navigated.

The two instruments are designed to be used in sequence. Orientation 2089 establishes what the student believes. ARIA tests whether those beliefs hold when they have consequences.

## Classroom notes

**Time:** approximately 10–15 minutes for the instrument itself. Discussion after is open-ended.

**Grade:** Grades 11–12. KMK Themenfeld — The Individual and Society / Science and Technology.

**Facilitation:** No teacher framing required before students begin. The instrument is self-contained. The most productive classroom moment is after the results screen — specifically the "someone who'd disagree" prompt, which surfaces the sharpest disagreements in the room.

**Sequencing:** Run Orientation 2089 before ARIA. The ARIA handoff is built into the end of this instrument.

**The forced-choice questions:** Five of the twenty-seven situations offer no middle option. These are the questions where the underlying values are most exposed. If students resist the forced choice, that resistance is itself worth discussing.
